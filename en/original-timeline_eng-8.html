City of David - Timeline

The Canaanite Period: 2210 from Creation 1550 BCE The Jebusite City Jerusalem as a Jebusite City is first mentioned in the Book of Joshua (15:8) and later in the Book of Judges in the story of the concubine of Givah (19:10): "But the man did not wish to stay, and he rose to leave. He finally arrived in the vicinity of Jebus, that is, Jerusalem". Jebusite Jerusalem remained a gentile enclave located between the inheritance of the tribe of Judah in the South and the inheritance of the tribe of Benjamin in the North. We learn about the might of the Jebusite city from the terraced stone structure located on the Eastern slope of the City of David hill (picture). This structure apparently supported the city’s fortress, the Fortress of Zion (Samuel II, 5:7).

In the time of Herod, while under Roman rule (1st century BCE), Jerusalem reached the peak of its prosperity. The massive construction that took place in the city is described in the books of Yoseph ben Matityahu [Josephus Flavius]. During the first century ACE, it seems that a number of palaces were built in the City of David for the royal family of Chadayev, however no remnants of these palaces have yet to be discovered. A few years following the death of Herod, the Romans installed a governor to rule over Judea. The year 66 ACE inaugurated the outbreak of revolution against Roman authority and the city was placed under siege. In the year 70 CE the walls were penetrated and the Second Temple went up in flames.
